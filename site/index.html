Most AI product manager roles want judgment about AI, not a model-building background.

Every week, I pull every product, program and technical program manager opening from 36 AI and tech companies and have a decision model label each one. Code handles the facts (location, pay); Jev judges the meaning (is the product AI, is ML required, is it a program or product role).

How it works

  1. 1
    Fetch. Pull every open role from 36 public Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever boards. Keep titles with "product" or "program".
  2. 2
    Facts in code. Pay ranges, the remote flag and Toronto/Canada location fit come from regex.
  3. 3
    Meaning from Jev. Six typed questions per posting (role kind, AI scope, seniority, ML required, hands-on AI skills required, engineering required), each with a probability.
  4. 4
    Buckets in code. Code combines the answers into buckets. Anything under 0.6 confidence goes to review instead of guessing.
An early version asked the model for location. It labeled Vancouver roles as US-only, so location moved to code.

How good are the labels?

I labeled 40 postings by hand and compared them with Jev. The question that matters most for this page is whether a role belongs in an AI bucket at all.

32 of 36agreed on AI bucket or not (89%). The other 4 went to review.
15 of 18on the half I labeled blind, before seeing Jev's answers

Seniority was within one level on all 40. "AI or not" merges AI-native and AI-platform, which is how the buckets use it; the three-way answer matched 18 of 40. The set is stratified by bucket, so these numbers describe each bucket, not a random posting. Full eval and disagreements.
